## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
We propose Farm2Market, a digital marketplace that helps farmers and FPOs list available produce and connect with nearby consumers, retailers, restaurants, and bulk buyers.

Farmers can add details such as crop type, quantity, quality, harvest date, and expected price. Buyers can browse available produce, compare listings, place orders, and arrange delivery. The platform can show indicative market prices to help farmers make informed decisions and support transparent negotiation.

A logistics module can help group nearby orders and connect sellers with transport providers. AI-based demand forecasting can use past sales, seasonal patterns, and local demand to estimate which produce may be needed and when. Route optimization can suggest efficient delivery routes to save time and fuel.

What makes the idea useful: it brings produce listings, buyer discovery, price information, order coordination, and logistics support into one simple platform designed for farmers and local buyers.

## Technical Approach
The platform can be developed as a mobile-friendly application with separate interfaces for farmers, buyers, and logistics providers.
- User access: Phone-number registration and role-based accounts for farmers, buyers, and transport providers.
- Produce listings: Forms to add crop, quantity, quality, price, location, and availability.
- Marketplace and orders: Search and filters for buyers, with order placement and status tracking.
- Price information: A dashboard showing available market-price data and recent platform listings.
- Demand forecasting: A machine-learning model can estimate demand using historical orders, seasonality, and local trends.
- Route planning: Mapping and routing services can group deliveries and suggest efficient routes.
- Notifications: SMS or in-app alerts for new orders, payment updates, and delivery status.
- Accessibility: A simple interface with regional-language support and clear icons can make the platform easier to use.
Implementation flow: Farmer lists produce → buyers discover and order produce → seller confirms the order → logistics are arranged → produce is delivered → order and sales data help improve future forecasts.

## Feasibility and Viability
The project is feasible as a prototype because its main functions—user accounts, produce listings, search, order management, notifications, and map-based routing—can be built using commonly available web and mobile technologies.

The first version can focus on one district and a small number of crops. This keeps onboarding and logistics manageable while the team gathers feedback from farmers, FPOs, and buyers. Demand forecasting can begin with basic seasonal and historical data, then improve as the platform receives more orders.

Potential challenges and responses:
- Limited digital familiarity: Provide a simple interface, regional-language options, and assisted onboarding through FPOs or local coordinators.
- Unreliable or incomplete listings: Use clear listing fields and allow buyers to confirm quality and quantity before finalizing orders.
- Transport availability: Start with local transport partners and group nearby orders where practical.
- Trust between new buyers and sellers: Provide verified profiles, order records, transparent pricing, and a way to report issues.
- Changing demand and prices: Show when price information was last updated and treat forecasts as guidance rather than guaranteed prices.
The platform can be sustained through small transaction fees, logistics coordination charges, or subscription services for larger buyers, while keeping basic farmer access affordable.

## Impact and Benefits
- For farmers: More ways to reach buyers, improved access to price information, and the potential to receive a larger share of the final sale price.
- For consumers: Better access to locally available produce and the potential for lower prices through shorter supply chains.
- For bulk buyers: Easier discovery of available produce, quantities, and suppliers.
- For logistics providers: More visibility into delivery demand and opportunities to combine nearby orders.
- For communities: Stronger local market connections and improved access to fresh produce.
- For the environment: Better delivery planning may reduce unnecessary travel, while improved matching of supply and demand may help limit avoidable food waste.
The actual benefits will depend on local adoption, reliable logistics, accurate listings, and fair pricing.
